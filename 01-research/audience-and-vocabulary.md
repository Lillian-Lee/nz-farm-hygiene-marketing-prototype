# Research: NZ Farm-Hygiene Audience, Vocabulary & Evidence Map

> **Scope note:** This is research for a **fictional portfolio brand ("HerdGuard")**. It is **not** affiliated with any real company. All product claims below are drafted for a made-up product and are flagged where they would require substantiation before real-world use.

## 1. Who the audience is

The primary audience for a NZ farm-hygiene ecommerce store splits into two segments found in the NZ farm-hygiene market:

**Dairy farmers and farm staff (primary).** Owner-operators, sharemilkers, herd managers and milk-harvesting staff on pasture-based dairy farms (Waikato, Canterbury, Southland, Taranaki). They buy milking-time hygiene consumables — teat spray, teat wipes, dairy detergents/sanitisers — usually in bulk, and care about milk-quality grading and cow comfort.

**Poultry / mixed-livestock operators and lifestyle blocks (secondary).** Commercial poultry sheds and smaller operations that need cleaning, disinfection and biosecurity products (footbaths, broad-spectrum disinfectants), heightened by ongoing avian-influenza (HPAI) awareness.

A third, commercially important audience is **rural retailers / dealers**, since NZ farm-hygiene suppliers commonly sell through rural dealers. The prototype is written for the end-farmer but the SEO and content plan keeps "stockist / dealer enquiry" as a conversion path.

## 2. Customer vocabulary (use their words)

Language collected from public NZ industry sources (DairyNZ, MPI). Using the audience's own terms is the single biggest credibility lever in this market.

Dairy / milking hygiene:

- **Teat spray** (not "teat disinfectant solution") — the everyday term.
- **Emollient** — the skin-conditioning ingredient; farmers judge sprays on emollient level. Best practice is **15–20% emollient in the final mix**, higher during high-challenge times.
- **Teat condition / teat health** — "smooth skin, no cracks or scabs, soft and supple". Problem thresholds farmers recognise: >5% of cows with sores, >10% with dry skin, >20% with rough teat-ends.
- **Mastitis** — the outcome everyone is trying to avoid. Teat spraying after every milking is shown to **reduce new mastitis infections by ~50%** (DairyNZ).
- **SCC / BMSCC** — (bulk milk) somatic cell count; the number that drives milk-quality grading and payment penalties.
- **Cups off / milking routine / lactation** — timing language ("teat spray immediately after cups come off, every cow, every milking, all lactation").
- **Strategic washing** — washing only visibly dirty teats; the common NZ practice.
- **RTU vs concentrate** — ready-to-use vs mix-your-own; a real purchase decision.

Poultry / general biosecurity:

- **Biosecurity** — the umbrella word.
- **Cleaning & disinfection (C&D)** — the standard shed-hygiene phrase.
- **Footbath** — entry-point hygiene at shed doors and farm gates.
- **Broad-spectrum disinfectant / DEFRA-type approval** — efficacy language buyers look for.
- **HPAI / avian influenza / bird flu** — the current risk driving poultry hygiene spend.

## 3. Recurring problems the audience is trying to solve

1. **Mastitis and high somatic cell counts** costing grade penalties and treatment — the number-one dairy pain point.
2. **Poor teat condition** (cracked, chapped teats in spring/wind/mud) that raises infection risk and slows milking.
3. **Inconsistent teat-spray coverage** — "poorly done on many farms"; farmers want a product and routine that make full coverage easy.
4. **Disease incursion** in poultry/mixed operations — keeping people, vehicles and equipment from carrying pathogens in.
5. **Buying friction** — knowing dilution rates, dose per cow/shed, and whether a product is RTU or concentrate; wanting a stockist nearby.

## 4. Claims that would need evidence before real-world use

For the portfolio, product copy is written to be *believable and correctly hedged*. In a real launch each of the following would need substantiation (lab data, registration, or a cited independent study) — they are deliberately **not** stated as proven facts in the prototype:

| Claim used carefully in copy | Evidence that would be required |
|---|---|
| "Helps reduce new mastitis infections" | Only stated as a benefit of *good teat-spraying practice per DairyNZ*, not as a product-specific proven result. |
| "15–20% emollient for teat conditioning" | Formulation spec / certificate of analysis for the actual product. |
| "Broad-spectrum / kills X% of pathogens" | Independent efficacy testing and any required NZ registration (ACVM/MPI). |
| "Trusted by NZ farmers" / testimonials | Real, permissioned customer quotes. |
| Specific dilution and coverage figures | Manufacturer's validated label directions. |
